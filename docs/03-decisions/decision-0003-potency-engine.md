# Decision 0003: The potency engine

Date: 2026-10-20

*Update: the active product constants were changed in decision 0004. The formulas and rules below are unchanged; the numbers quoted in "One consequence worth knowing" describe the original team-document constants, which are now the reference profile.*

This records how the box computes how much of a medicine's strength is left, and why it is built the way it is. The maths follows the master brief (section 3.2) and amendments A5 and A6. Potency is a model output for an imaginary product with assumed constants, not a measurement.

## The formulas

Temperatures go in as Celsius and are converted to kelvin inside the code (add 273.15). Times are in hours.

- **Acceleration factor:** AF = exp( (Ea/R) x (1 / T_ref - 1 / T) ). It is exactly 1 at the reference temperature (5 C), above 1 when warmer, below 1 when colder.
- **Equivalent time at the reference temperature:** tau_eq = the sum of AF x dt over all intervals. One hour at a high temperature "costs" many reference hours.
- **Potency:** P = exp( -k_ref x tau_eq ), with k_ref = ln(1 / P_min) / tau_max. By construction P equals P_min exactly when tau_eq reaches tau_max.
- **Shelf life used:** tau_eq / tau_max. The team document's "0.110 percent" and "2.66 percent" are this value times 100.
- **Mean kinetic temperature (MKT):** the one steady temperature that would do the same damage. It is weighted by how long each temperature lasted (amendment A6) and is computed in a scaled form so very low temperatures cannot underflow to zero.
- **Hours to minimum potency at a temperature:** (tau_max - tau_eq) / AF. This is a helper of mine for the later planner. The brief defines the remaining life at the reference temperature (tau_max - tau_eq); this divides it by AF to turn it into real hours at the current temperature. It is not a formula from the brief.

## Where the constants come from

`Ea/R = 10000 K`, the 5 C reference, the 24 month (17,520 h) shelf life and `P_min = 0.90` are product defaults from the team's document in `config/product_default.yaml`, for an imaginary 2 to 8 C vaccine. They are not measured facts. `k_ref` is computed from them, never stored twice.

## One consequence worth knowing

With these constants, potency drops to P_min only when the whole 24 month shelf life has been spent. The worst scenario in the simulator (a 36 hour cooling failure in a very hot cargo area) spends about 9 percent of the shelf life and leaves potency near 99 percent. So "potency below P_min" will only appear for much longer or hotter journeys. The meaningful number on screen for short journeys is "shelf life used".

## Right-endpoint integration

Each reading stands for the time since the previous reading, and that whole interval is integrated with the temperature of the later reading. The brief does not choose a rule. For a constant temperature every rule gives the same answer, so the worked examples are unaffected. The right endpoint is the natural choice for a streaming program because it never needs a reading it has not received yet.

## The suspect-reading rule (amendment A5)

Freezing and heat damage are not Arrhenius effects, so readings outside 0 C to 40 C are handled separately.

1. The first reading outside the limits is **suspect**. It is left out of the maths: its interval is integrated with the last good temperature (hold-last-valid) and it counts in "samples excluded".
2. When the configured number of readings in a row are outside the limits (2 by default, read from config) the excursion is **confirmed**. The earlier suspect readings are then counted as real, with their own temperatures, exactly once and without double counting. They leave the excluded count.
3. A reading back inside the limits returns the status to "ok". A lone suspect reading stays excluded; a confirmed excursion stays in the total.

The undecided intervals are kept in a small pending list apart from the committed totals, so nothing is ever subtracted and nothing can be counted twice. Until a decision is made, the reported state shows the suspect interval as held. If the brief had said something different about excluded readings, the brief would have won; it says only that a single extreme reading is marked suspect and excluded, and that quarantine needs consecutive samples, which is what the rule implements. The hold-last-valid treatment and the retroactive correction are my additions to fill the gaps.

## What the tracker sees

Only the reported readings. The tracker is also run over the true temperature from the ground-truth files, but only inside an analysis tool and in tests, to show what a falsified sensor hides.

## Test vectors

The expected numbers for a later TypeScript port live in `shared/test-vectors/potency_vectors.json`, generated by the Python code. The only numbers typed by hand are the rounded worked examples from the team document, checked at the document's own precision.
